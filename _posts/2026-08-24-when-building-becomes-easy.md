---
title: "When Building Becomes Easy, the Hard Part Moves Elsewhere"
date: 2026-08-24
categories: [startup, product, building]
---

This post is an English translation of a piece originally written for our internal newsletter.<br>
<br>
Over the past three years, I have had far more experiences of things that used to feel impossible suddenly becoming possible. It feels as though the center of gravity in early-stage development has shifted.<br>
<br>
When development itself was expensive, the question before building an MVP was how simply we could solve a problem and how much satisfaction we could create for the other person. But now it is different. As the cost of early-stage development falls, the question becomes how deliberately we can move, step by step, toward something farther away.<br>
<br>
We do not have to confine ourselves to chasing numbers with problems that are easy to solve. What matters more and more is whether we are steadily climbing toward the highest point—and whether there are fans who want to climb with us.<br>
<br>
Working with many different companies is genuinely valuable, and it has taught us a great deal. But we also need to take on difficult challenges ourselves and accumulate many more experiences.<br>
<br>
Of course, that alone does not create an advantage. Having the knowledge and experience to understand the whole picture remains an advantage, and in that sense senior people are still structurally advantaged. That has not changed. Development that looks all the way through to scalable production, or to the final product, cannot be mastered overnight.<br>
<br>
At least when viewed on the larger scale of a life, unless we set our sights firmly on substantial challenges and goals, we may be overwhelmed by ever-larger models.<br>
<br>
What, then, is the meaning of fighting together as a startup? To me, it is speed, being at the frontier, expertise, and an exchange with the knowledge that already exists. When we offer what we can do, the other side brings back knowledge woven through history.<br>
<br>
I am sure none of the time we are spending now will be wasted. Rather, let us keep running toward something more difficult and more meaningful.<br>
