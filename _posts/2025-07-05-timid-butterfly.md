This post is an English translation of an essay originally written in Japanese.

Still carried along by my run around the Imperial Palace, I run through Nihonbashi. I'm not used to these humid summer nights in the city. The air shows no sign of cooling even after sunset, and my body is still hot from running. My T-shirt, darkened with sweat, clings to me and won't come away.

Along the way, a tourist who looks to be visiting from abroad is pointing a camera at a street tree. I glance over and see a blue butterfly. When I travel abroad, I too get curious about insects I've never seen and find myself watching them for ages. Thinking that must be how they feel, I remember a butterfly I saw in Australia.

Everything I'd made it to the final round for ended in rejection. It feels like getting a message from your partner saying “Let's break up” while you're in the middle of a marathon. Before sadness comes, there's “I'm tired. I'll think about it later.”

I'm shocked, but honestly, stopping the marathon here hasn't even crossed my mind. “These things happen,” I think, taking another ragged breath. There's no water station in sight. Is there even one?

From the butterfly's point of view, it must be awful. To have been born into the middle of a city this hot and humid, with no knowing whether there's even water to drink. People may stop to admire it, but that doesn't bring the river any closer.

A butterfly doesn't fly with the precision of a dragonfly, or sing like a cicada. There seems to be something dazed about the way it flutters.

Perhaps that butterfly was timid, deep down. I have no way of knowing. That blue thing, gleaming in the city, was beautiful.
